## News roundup, Sep 15 2026 (Instagram carousel, approved and published)

**Anthropic Researcher Resigns, Says AI Labs Are "Gambling With Our Lives"**
Jacob Coxon spent three years training AI at OpenAI and Anthropic. On Sep 8 he resigned and wrote
that both companies are **"gambling with our lives."** His posts reached more than 100 million people
overnight. His ask: the companies agree to slow down before their AI starts building the next
version of itself.

**Anthropic Safety Lead: Odds AI "Could Kill All Humans" Are Over 10%**
Evan Hubinger, who leads a safety team at Anthropic, replied publicly: **"we really do earnestly
believe AI could kill all humans! I personally think it is >10% within the next decade."** The same
day, OpenAI added Paul Christiano to the committee that decides whether its new models ship. He
wrote there is "a meaningful risk that rapid acceleration in AI capabilities leads to catastrophic
and irreversible loss of control in the very near term."

**Two More Safety Researchers Quit Anthropic & DeepMind**
Joe Benton (Anthropic) and Josh Engels (Google DeepMind) resigned this week to join METR, an
independent group that investigates AI incidents. **"There are no adults in the room,"** Engels told
NBC News. **"People are trying their best, but there is no one coming to save us."** Benton: nearly
all company transparency about these risks "is entirely voluntary." Three frontier-lab resignations
in one week.

**Senators From Both Parties Move on AI Risk After Researcher's Warning**
Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) said on ABC's The View that AI poses a **"catastrophic risk,"** called Coxon's
post **"highly concerning,"** and said he is drafting legislation with Sens. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) and
John Thune (R-SD). Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) is convening a closed-door briefing for senators of
both parties on Sep 16 with AI pioneer Geoffrey Hinton.

**Anthropic Newest AI Is Nearing the Bioweapons Threshold**
Anthropic's Sep 10 threat report says its newer models are **"no longer comfortably below"** the level
where they could help someone build a biological weapon. The company says it disrupted five cases of
misuse for dangerous pathogen research, including one tied to a military institute.

**Only 1 in 6 Voters Want AI Development to Speed Up**
A national poll released Sep 10 asked voters to choose among three paths for advanced AI: **half
chose slowing development with government able to set the pace, a third chose a full pause, and
about 1 in 6 chose speeding up.** Nearly 6 in 10 said the government should already be requiring AI
companies to slow down.
